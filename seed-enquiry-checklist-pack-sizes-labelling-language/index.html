Seed enquiry checklist: specify retail pack sizes and labelling language

What packaging and labelling information should a reseller include in a seed enquiry?

A clear seed enquiry keeps three records apart: the products and catalogue quantities being requested, the retail packaging and labelling requirements, and the delivery details. This distinction matters because a catalogue quantity and unit do not by themselves confirm the pack size or labelling language needed for resale.

Recording each item precisely gives the supplier a clear basis for checking what can be supplied. An enquiry is non-binding, and any arrangements are confirmed in the quotation. Keep the destination details separate as well: https://bulkseedsinternational.com/en/georeferencing-for-smarter-b2b-vegetable-seed-selection/ explains why the destination country and postal code are included when shipping options and documents for the proposed shipment are reviewed.

Six details to record for the enquiry

Include the following information for every seed item:

  1. Product identity: the botanical name, variety and catalogue reference.
  2. Catalogue quantity and unit: the quantity required for each item and the unit used in the catalogue request. Including the unit also helps distinguish similarly named varieties.
  3. Intended retail pack size: the requested pack size and the unit used to define it. Record this separately from the catalogue quantity and unit rather than treating the catalogue unit as the retailer’s packaging requirement.
  4. Required labelling language: the language that should appear on the labels. If the requirement differs between products, repeat it with each relevant product instead of leaving it as a general note.
  5. Destination: the destination country and postal code.
  6. Requested delivery date: the date that should be included in the review of the proposed quotation and shipment.

Include the business contact details as well. Where practical, repeat the pack size and labelling language on each product line so that neither requirement becomes detached from the correct variety.

Why catalogue quantities and retail pack sizes need separate lines

A structured enquiry separates three practical questions:

  • Product record: Which variety, catalogue reference, quantity and catalogue unit are being requested?
  • Packaging record: Which retail pack size and labelling language should be reviewed?
  • Delivery record: Where should the shipment go, and by which date?

Available pack sizes are confirmed in the quotation. For that reason, present the pack size and labelling language as requested requirements, not as arrangements that have already been agreed. This also prevents a catalogue unit from being mistaken for the unit of a finished retail pack.

A copy-ready enquiry structure

The following format keeps the key information visible without adding assumptions about which products or pack sizes are available:

  • Product: [botanical name], [variety], catalogue reference [reference]
  • Catalogue request: [quantity] [catalogue unit]
  • Retail packaging: [requested pack size] [pack-size unit]
  • Labelling: [required labelling language]
  • Destination: [country], [postal code]
  • Requested delivery date: [date]
  • Business contact: [name] and [business contact details]

Repeat the product, packaging and labelling lines for each item. If several products are grouped under the same pack specification, show every catalogue reference so that the connection between the products and that specification remains clear.

A final check before sending

  • Does every item have a botanical name, variety and catalogue reference?
  • Does every requested quantity have a unit?
  • Are the retail pack size and its unit recorded separately from the catalogue request?
  • Is the required labelling language stated for each relevant item?
  • Are the destination country, postal code and requested delivery date included?
  • Are the business contact details included?
  • Are pack sizes and labelling arrangements presented as requests rather than confirmed terms?

A quick test is whether someone reading the enquiry can tell which product is involved, how much is being requested, what retail pack and labelling language should be reviewed, and where and when delivery is intended.

What to check in the quotation

Submitting an enquiry does not place an order. The supplier reviews the selection and checks availability, prices and shipping conditions before making an offer. For this enquiry, read the quotation to see which pack sizes have been confirmed and what arrangements have been stated for the requested labelling language and delivery date. Do not assume that a requested pack size or labelling arrangement is available unless it is addressed in the quotation.
